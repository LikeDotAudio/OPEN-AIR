15# Factory to create custom block instances. Used only in reconfigurable devices.
16# The idea is that you instantiate a factory once, populate it with
17# proto-objects and proto-signal paths, then use it subsequently to instantiate
18# identical blocks. In this context, **proto-object** means a prototype of a
19# block member. Unbound objects are identified by **proto-object numbers
20# (PONo's)** instead of actual object numbers. PONos are unique within the
21# factory, and are converted to globally unique object numbers ONos) in all new
22# block instances that the factory builds. Correspondingly, **proto-signal
23# path** means a signal path expressed in terms of **PONos** rather than
24# **ONos**. When the factory constructs a block, it converts all of its unbound
25# signal paths to normal (bound) signal paths by mapping its **PONos** into
26# **ONos**. The factory also holds a list of **proto-blockports** which are
27# **OcaPorts** that are allocated to new blocks the factory builds. As well, the
28# proto-objects in the factory may contain lists of their own proto-ports.
29# Together, the factory's proto-ports and its members' proto-ports are used to
30# define the factory's set of proto-signal paths. Factories may be predefined at
31# time of device manufacture, or constructed "on the fly" by controllers. To
32# **create a factory**, the controller calls a block's **CreateMember(...)**
33# method with the **ClassID** of this class (**OcaBlockFactory**). Factories
34# ignore which block creates them, so it makes no difference which block's
35# **CreateMember(...)** method is used. It will usually make the most sense to
36# use the Root Block's method. To add proto-objects, proto-ports, and
37# proto-signal paths to a block factory, the controller calls the factory's
38# **DefineProtoMember(...), DefineProtoPort(...), and
39# DefineProtoSignalPath(...**) methods, respectively.
